## Recommendation

- **Alert rule:** failure probability >= **0.5** for **2** consecutive cycle(s).
- Decision data: train engines, out-of-fold (70 engines). Cost ratio used: 10:1 (assumption). Safety constraint (no engine missed or warned < 10 cycles ahead, and still true for the next 2 higher thresholds) met: True.
- **Robust choice:** the rule is stably safe in all 5 model variants tested (logistic regression with C in [0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0]); worst-case shortest warning across variants: 14 cycles.
- On the decision engines (base fit) this rule gives 0 missed, 0 too late, 5 far-premature alarms (alert more than 39 cycles before failure), minimum warning 14 cycles; the current default (0.5, k=1) gives 0 missed/late and 10 far-premature.
- Held-out confirmation on 15 validation engines: 0 missed, 0 too late, 1 far-premature; minimum warning 19 cycles; row-level precision 0.868, recall 0.947, F1 0.906 (default 0.5, k=1: 0 missed/late, 2 far-premature; precision 0.828, recall 0.965, F1 0.892).

## Assumptions (to be confirmed with the client / team)

- Horizon 24 cycles (cycles, not hours; provisional).
- Minimum useful notice 10 cycles; alerts up to 39 cycles before failure are acceptable early warnings.
- Cost of an unplanned failure vs one unnecessary maintenance halt is unknown; the brief only says downtime is far more expensive and false alarms are costly. Ratios of 5:1 to 50:1 are shown.

## Cost of the two error types

| Error | What happens | Cost | What the data show |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missed failure (never alerted, or alerted too late to act) | Unplanned stop, no time to schedule maintenance | Very high (the brief: downtime costs millions) | Rare here; appears only at high thresholds / long persistence rules |
| False alarm (alert far ahead of failure) | Unnecessary maintenance halt, wasted remaining life | Moderate each, but accumulates across a fleet | Frequent at low thresholds with k = 1 (flickering probabilities) |

## Limitations

- Simulated turbofan data; cycles are not hours; the 24-cycle horizon is provisional.
- A handful of engines drive the result (one hard engine decides where the safety cliff sits); treat the threshold as provisional.
- Row-level F1 and engine-level cost point in somewhat different directions; the engine view is closer to operations.
- Thresholds are model-specific. The deployed GRU needs its own analysis (use `--model gru_artifact`).
- The reserved test split was not used.
